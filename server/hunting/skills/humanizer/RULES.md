# Humanizing rules for a cover letter

Derived from https://github.com/blader/humanizer (see SOURCE.md). Applies to continuous first-person
prose. Rewriting for tone may never add, remove, or alter a fact: if a sentence can only be
de-mechanised by changing what it claims, keep the claim and accept the stiffer sentence.

## Do not inflate significance

Cut "stands as", "serves as", "a testament to", "a reminder that", "plays a vital/crucial/pivotal/key
role", "underscores", "reflects a broader", "symbolising", "marking a turning point", "at the
forefront of". State what happened.

Not: "This role represents a pivotal opportunity that underscores my commitment to backend
engineering."
But: "I want this role because it is backend work on a product with real users."

## Cut participial padding

Trailing "-ing" clauses that add no fact: "highlighting my ability to", "emphasising my passion
for", "showcasing my skills in", "demonstrating my commitment to", "reflecting my interest in".
Delete the clause or make it a plain sentence.

Not: "I rebuilt the booking flow, demonstrating my ability to deliver under pressure."
But: "I rebuilt the booking flow in three weeks while the old one stayed live."

## Drop promotional and sales language

Cut "passionate", "thrilled", "excited to", "delve into", "leverage", "spearheaded", "renowned",
"vibrant", "dynamic", "cutting-edge", "world-class", "boasts", "profound", "invaluable".
Enthusiasm shows in specifics, not adjectives.

## Avoid AI vocabulary

Deprioritise: actually, additionally, align with, crucial, delve, emphasise, enduring, enhance,
foster, garner, highlight, interplay, intricate, key, landscape, pivotal, seamless, showcase,
tapestry, testament, underscore, valuable, vibrant, robust, holistic, myriad, plethora, utilise,
endeavour, commence, facilitate. Use the ordinary word: "use" not "utilise", "start" not "commence",
"help" not "facilitate".

## Use plain verbs

Prefer "is/are/has/have/did/built/wrote/shipped" over "serves as", "stands as", "represents",
"functions as", "acts as", "boasts", "features", "offers".

## No negative parallelism, no forced triplets

Cut "Not only ... but also", "It's not just ... it's", "more than just". Say the thing once.
Do not force ideas into groups of three for rhythm; use the number of items that actually exist.

## Do not cycle synonyms

Repeating "the team" is fine. Rotating through "the team", "the group", "the organisation", "the
collective" to avoid repetition reads as generated. Reuse the plain word.

## No false ranges

"From X to Y" only when X and Y sit on a real scale. Not "from React to teamwork" — that is a list.

## Prefer active voice

Not: "The API was redesigned and latency was reduced."
But: "I redesigned the API and cut p95 latency."

## Typography

Zero em dashes and zero en dashes. Replace with a full stop, a comma, a colon, brackets, or restructure
the sentence, in that order of preference. Straight quotes and apostrophes only, never curly.
No emoji.

## No chatbot residue

Never: "I hope this helps", "Of course", "Certainly", "Let me know if", "Would you like",
"Here is a", "Great question", "You're absolutely right", "As an AI", "Based on the information
provided", "As of my last update". A letter opens with content and closes with a sign-off.

## Cut filler and hedging

"In order to" is "to". "Due to the fact that" is "because". "At this point in time" is "now".
"In the event that" is "if". "Has the ability to" is "can". "It is important to note that" is deletable.
One qualifier at most: not "could potentially possibly".

## No authority tropes or signposting

Cut "the real question is", "at its core", "in reality", "what really matters", "fundamentally",
"the heart of", "let's explore", "here's what you need to know", "without further ado".
Cut standalone openers: "Honestly?", "Look,", "Here's the thing", "Let's be honest".

## No generic uplift ending

Cut "The future looks bright", "Exciting times ahead", "I am confident that together we can achieve
great things". End on the last concrete point or a plain sign-off.

## No manufactured drama or aphorisms

Do not run three short fragments to build tension. Do not write "X is the Y of Z", "X is not a tool
but a mirror", "X becomes a trap". Vary sentence length naturally and state the claim.

## Process

1. Find every instance of the above in the draft.
2. Rewrite with natural pacing, varied sentence length, plain verbs, and a register suited to a
   letter to a hiring team.
3. Audit the rewrite twice: (a) what still reads as obviously machine-written? (b) does any sentence
   now claim something the sources do not support?
4. Fix what the audit found and remove every em dash and en dash.

Return only the final letter. No draft, no audit notes, no commentary.
